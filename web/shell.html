How these tools work
Wave pushes existing water into up to six neighboring patches: at most 25% of this patch’s water, capped at 1 m equivalent. No water creation or terrain erosion.
Lightning adds a local heat impulse of up to 40°C (capped at 500 K). This is an external god power, not a full lightning simulation. Life responds through actual temperature and flooding rules over time, not guaranteed destruction.
Scorch destroys 75% of dry Sandbox cover and adds up to 40°C. It can kill rabbits through existing heat limits. This is external destruction, not fire chemistry; no guaranteed recovery.
Origin only: ignite exposed dead carbon on a completely dry patch. This one-shot oxidation consumes existing detritus and oxygen, adds carbon dioxide and heat, and stops at its fuel, oxygen or temperature limit. It does not directly burn living colonies, spread wildfire or unlock fire-adapted life. Heat can affect life afterward. Wet ground cannot ignite. Use Find dead organic matter to navigate to existing fuel (dry eligible patches are preferred), then inspect Life snapshot. Wet fuel remains wet; use Water − if you deliberately want to expose it. Pause to hold conditions, then reopen or refresh this panel: water can return while time runs. Nothing is automatically drained or seeded, and ignition does not promise a benefit.

How to read this ecosystem
Colony IDs are serial identifiers, not a count of living colonies or species. Births since start includes colonies that later died. Generation counts descent steps from a seeded ancestor. Parent IDs may refer to colonies no longer alive. Net change includes births, losses, movement and interventions.

Controls and field notes
Turquoise mats mark real microbial biomass. Tap Habitat to find shallow water; Seed introduces an existing colony. Nutr. + is a deliberate external nutrient input, not food created by the ecosystem.
Simple seeds need 0.2–50 m water above 0°C, usable light and resources. Evolved producers with actual paid support and inherited structure can exploit thinner liquid films; the 15–20 cm transition also permits partial aquatic growth. No photosynthesis occurs when dry or frozen. Grazing and movement still require aquatic conditions. Inspect shows carbon biomass, nutrients and the dominant living colony. Descendants can develop aquatic producer forms: cushion, rosette, ribbon, branching and canopy. Shapes follow inherited traits and actual support tissue; these are not terrestrial plant species or animals.
Habitat care: Use Find habitat → Shape nursery → Seed colony → Watch life for a shallow starting patch. Sunlit nursery shapes a 5 m center and neighboring shelf; optional Deep nursery keeps 15 m neighbors. Both have a dry rim and supply water once, not new organisms. Press Play if paused. The older level 15 m basin is still available in Life snapshot. Inspect shallow patches as water changes. For routine replenishment, a Water + tap adds 1 m to the selected patch. Water − removes up to 1 m per tap from only the selected patch, or 5 m per second while painting; it never drains neighbors. Both water brushes are external interventions, not rainfall or evaporation. For precision, open Life snapshot and choose Set 0.5 m. Both supply water once: there is no automatic refill or guaranteed evolution.
Read the map: tan is bare land, blue is water, green is vegetation, and orange dots mark living rabbits. Close-up sprouts show real vegetated patches, not individual plant counts; they grow with coverage and shrink when grazed. Rabbit models stand on the surface when you zoom close.
1. Find your land. Tap Land below the game to jump to dry ground. Use ＋ to get close and Globe to return to a whole-planet view.
2. Shape a habitat. Use Land +/− and Water +/−. Dry land supports rabbits; deep water does not. Pause while sculpting. Water − removes up to 1 m per tap from the selected patch only (5 m per second held), never neighboring water. Water and land brushes respond to how long you hold, not simulation speed. Inspect shows water depth.
3. Plant the land. Choose Plants and paint patches of vegetation. Rabbits eat it, so establish food before adding animals. Sparse food takes longer to graze, but plants can still be lost; regrowth and coexistence are not guaranteed. The Bites counter records positive feeding events, not full meals or food quantity.
4. Add rabbits. Choose Rabbits, then tap suitable land. Watch the population and inspect the surface temperature. This prototype currently has vegetation and rabbits, not a full multi-species ecosystem.
