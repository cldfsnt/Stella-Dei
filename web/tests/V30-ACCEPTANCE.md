# v30: life in shallower water

A bounded evolutionary step, not a completed terrestrial ecosystem. Origin producers with both inherited structure and actual living support tissue can use shallower liquid water. Tissue still costs resources and maintenance. There is no added water reservoir, free biomass, timed unlock or guaranteed survival.

## What changes
- Producer photosynthesis now depends on actual depth and functional support below 20 cm. A smooth 15–20 cm transition permits some aquatic growth without implying adaptation.
- As water and support approach zero, protection approaches dry behavior continuously. Independent review caught and corrected an earlier microscopic-water survival loophole before release.
- No photosynthesis occurs in fully dry or frozen conditions. Seed actions, discovery, green targets and click revalidation consistently require water above 0°C.
- Life inspection explains current suitability and the possibility of growth or decline. Existing grazing/movement retains its older aquatic temperature rules; this is not a complete freezing model.

## Actual evolution evidence
Two paired 100-day native worlds began from an ordinary nursery and one simple seed, with no later care, assigned traits or outcome-driven retuning. Naturally descended supported producers persisted in roughly 9–13 cm water. Conservative bookkeeping identified five propagules funded by credit earned below 15 cm. Only one birth itself occurred below 15 cm; the others occurred at 15–17 cm. Every specifically traced offspring was absent by day 100. This demonstrates persistent shallow producers and exploratory reproduction, not established dryland offspring, vascular plants or evolved land animals.

The new model produced more biomass in both tested worlds, not a universal advantage. Resource costs and adverse conditions remain. Local carbon/nutrient tests pass within floating-point tolerance; whole-climate water loss remains in both experimental/control worlds and is disclosed in the evidence. No exact whole-planet conservation claim.
